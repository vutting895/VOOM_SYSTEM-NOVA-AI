## 1. Executive decision

VOOM should evolve the Internal Quant Engine into the **Quantitative Intelligence & Research Engine**. The Deep Research Core becomes the primary intelligence workflow. The Quant Engine remains the deterministic computation and evaluation plane that tests hypotheses generated by research.

This is one connected research system, not a second broker gateway, a general-purpose autonomous agent, or an unbounded self-improving trading bot.

### Product vocabulary

| Name | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Quantitative Intelligence & Research Engine** | The overall product capability: research planning, evidence, quantitative experiments, evaluation and review |
| **Deep Research Core** | Builds research plans, discovers and records sources, maintains claims/evidence, forms falsifiable hypotheses and compiles experiments |
| **Quant Compute Engine** | Validates datasets, builds point-in-time features, runs strategy/simulation plugins, evaluates results and writes reproducible artifacts |
| **NOVA System Integration Hub** | Authenticates users, resolves tenant/resource ownership, authorizes tools, creates jobs and records audit correlation |
| **Research Window** | User-facing view of plans, evidence, experiment progress, results, limitations and human review |

### Non-goals

- No broker order, position, transfer, withdrawal or account-setting write capability.
- No LLM authority over user identity, tenant ownership, broker routing, deterministic risk limits or candidate promotion.
- No production training service, model registry, feature store, orchestration framework, queue, object bucket or database migration in this architecture-only milestone.
- No claim that a listed Learning Universe track or prototype is already operational.

          |
          v

          |
          v

### Boundary rules

1. The Hub is the only application authorization boundary. The Deep Research Core receives a trusted, server-created scope context; it must not accept a model-generated owner, tenant or connection ID as authority.
2. Deep Research may plan and synthesize, but every external tool call must come from a server-side allowlist with bounded inputs and explicit permissions.
3. The LLM is a planner and summarizer, not the source of truth. Deterministic validation code owns schemas, access policy, experiment constraints and result acceptance.
4. The Quant Compute Worker accepts versioned datasets and experiment specifications. It never receives broker passwords, Bridge tokens, Vault plaintext or unrestricted broker credentials.
5. Compute results are immutable evidence. A report may explain a result but cannot change the underlying run, metrics or dataset.
6. Research approval can authorize a further paper/shadow evaluation stage only. It cannot enable broker execution.

## 4. Deep Research Core workflow

### 4.1 Research intake and plan generation

Convert a natural-language objective into a versioned plan containing:

- objective and bounded scope;
- primary research questions and decomposed sub-questions;
- market, symbols, timeframe, geographic/provider constraints and date range where applicable;
- source policy and preferred primary sources;
- assumptions, exclusions and definitions;
- measurable acceptance criteria and disconfirming evidence;
- estimated data requirements, resource budget and stop conditions;
- whether the work is explanatory research or should produce a Quant Experiment.

Ambiguous or missing material inputs must be requested rather than guessed. A plan revision creates a new version; previous versions remain inspectable.

### 4.2 Source discovery and snapshotting

Source adapters may cover approved official documentation, academic papers, repository files/commits/PRs, documented data providers and user-authorized files. Each adapter has its own retrieval and licensing rules.

Persist at least:

- canonical source URI and resolved URI;
- title, publisher/owner, source type and authority category;
- publication/update timestamp when supplied and retrieval timestamp;
- content type, parser/extractor version and extraction method;
- source snapshot SHA-256 and the exact passages used;
- license/retention constraints and retrieval warnings;
- relation to the research question and the claims it supports or contradicts.

Do not assume a search result snippet is equivalent to reading the source. Keep excerpts within licensing limits and preserve links to the primary source. When content cannot be inspected, mark it as unavailable/unverified.

### 4.3 Evidence Ledger

Every material claim in a report must point to one or more source snapshot IDs and exact passages, or be explicitly labelled as an inference or hypothesis.

Use explicit classifications:

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `observed` | Directly present in an inspected source, repository artifact or measured result |
| `corroborated` | Independently supported by multiple relevant sources |
| `inferred` | Reasoned interpretation whose premises are cited |
| `hypothesis` | A falsifiable proposition not yet established by evidence |
| `disputed` | Relevant sources or experiments conflict |
| `unsupported` | No adequate supporting evidence was found |
| `rejected` | Evidence or a valid experiment contradicts the claim |

Source authority, recency, relevance, method transparency and independent replication should be recorded as separate dimensions. Do not collapse them into a single opaque confidence number, and do not treat a retrieval score or model confidence as proof.

Retain negative findings, failed experiments, source conflicts and known unknowns. A research loop must not discard evidence merely because it conflicts with the current preferred hypothesis.

### 4.4 Hypothesis workspace

For each hypothesis, record:

- hypothesis statement and scope;
- supporting evidence IDs and counter-evidence IDs;
- competing explanations;
- observable outcome and target metric;
- null/baseline expectation;
- data/sample requirements and known limitations;
- planned test and evaluation windows;
- falsification rule, stop rule and review owner.

Promote a hypothesis to an experiment only when the test is capable of disproving it. A narrative that only seeks confirming examples is not a complete research plan.

### 4.5 Experiment compiler

Compile only a reviewed hypothesis into an immutable, typed Quant Experiment contract. The compiler may fill deterministic defaults that are explicitly defined in versioned policy; it must not invent missing source data or infer unprovided risk permissions.

The compiled experiment references the exact dataset manifest, strategy adapter, feature schema, code/runtime revision, parameter set, random seed, cost model, fold schedule, purge/embargo policy, baselines and evaluator version.

Changing any material input creates a new experiment identity. Never mutate an already completed run in place.

### 4.6 Research synthesis

The final research artifact contains:

1. question, scope, plan version and research date;
2. executive conclusion with evidence class for each key point;
3. source register and claim-to-evidence mapping;
4. counter-evidence, contradictions and unresolved questions;
5. hypothesis and experiment specifications;
6. quant results, baselines, cost assumptions, fold-level results and uncertainty;
7. failed/negative results and operational limitations;
8. reproducibility identifiers, dataset/code hashes and artifact links;
9. a human-review decision and its scope.

Research synthesis is not a forecast, investment guarantee, broker connection confirmation or trade instruction.

## 5. Typed contracts and state model

All contracts are versioned, validated on both the producing and consuming boundaries, reject unknown fields, and have explicit maximum sizes/counts.

### 5.1 Proposed contract families

| Contract | Required content |
|---|---|
| `nova.research-task.v1` | research ID, trusted owner/tenant scope, objective, question tree, constraints, source policy, plan version, status, timestamps, correlation/idempotency IDs |
| `nova.research-source.v1` | source ID, URI, title, publisher, source type, publication/access timestamps, snapshot hash, extraction/parser version, exact passage references, usage/retention notes |
| `nova.research-claim.v1` | claim ID/text, classification, source IDs, supporting/counter-evidence, scope, limitations and reviewer state |
| `nova.research-hypothesis.v1` | hypothesis ID, falsification rule, alternatives, observable outcome, data requirements, experiment references and status |
| `nova.quant-experiment.v1` | research ID, dataset manifest ID/hash, strategy/feature versions, code/runtime revision, parameters, seed, folds, purge/embargo, cost model, baselines and evaluator version |
| `nova.quant-run.v1` | run ID, experiment hash, status, timestamps, bounded error, metrics, artifact hashes/URIs, warnings and evaluator provenance |

Tenant and owner fields are server-authored and revalidated by the Hub. They must not be copied from untrusted prompt/model/source payloads.

### 5.2 Research task lifecycle

```text
draft -> planned -> collecting_sources -> evidence_ready
      -> synthesizing -> experiment_ready -> running
      -> evaluated -> human_review
      -> approved_for_paper_or_shadow | rejected | archived

Any active state may become blocked, failed or cancelled where policy permits.
```

Not every research task needs a quant run. A literature or architecture task can finish with an evidence-backed report. `experiment_ready` requires a falsifiable hypothesis and a complete experiment contract.

### 5.3 Quant run lifecycle

`queued -> running -> completed | failed | rejected | cancelled`

Only the worker may report computation status. Timeout, missing artifacts, incomplete folds or invalid output must not be reported as completed.

## 6. Quantitative evaluation standard

### 6.1 Data and point-in-time correctness

- Reject malformed, duplicated, out-of-order, timezone-naive, non-finite and impossible OHLCV rows. Report missing/irregular intervals separately; apply provider and instrument session calendars before treating every elapsed interval as a missing candle.
- Preserve raw-source provenance and immutable normalized snapshots. A quality warning must survive into every dependent experiment report.
- Separate event time from availability/created time. A feature, news item, fundamental value or corrected record can enter a historical decision only if it was actually available by that decision timestamp.
- Record corporate-action/contract-roll/session conventions where applicable. Do not use current constituent universes or backfilled revisions as if they were known historically.
- For pivot/structure indicators, record the confirmation delay. Earliest simulated entry follows the first eligible time the signal could actually have been observed.

### 6.2 Split and fold design

- Chronological train/validation/test partitions are mandatory for time-dependent market data.
- Purge size must derive from the maximum forward label horizon or overlapping outcome window, not an arbitrary convenience constant.
- Add embargo where the selected fold design requires it and record its exact boundaries.
- Final OOS is frozen before parameter/model selection and is never used to choose candidates.
- Walk-forward folds must document whether test windows overlap. Aggregation must not double-count overlapping bars/trades.
- Nested walk-forward is required when parameter/model selection occurs inside a walk-forward evaluation.

### 6.3 Simulator and costs

Include only execution assumptions supported by the dataset and simulator. Record spread, commission, slippage, funding/borrow, contract size, tick size, quote currency conversion, latency and partial-fill treatment where applicable.

An OHLCV-only bar simulator must not claim tick-level precision. Define candle-close signal timing, next-bar eligibility, stop/target precedence when both levels occur within a bar, gap handling and market-session behavior.

### 6.4 Required reporting

At minimum report trade count, net result after costs, expectancy/trade, profit factor, maximum drawdown, exposure, turnover, average win/loss and performance by time/regime/fold. State sampling and annualization conventions for Sharpe/Sortino and include stability/uncertainty ranges.

Track the full number of attempted trials, not only the winning parameter set. Use Probability of Backtest Overfitting / CSCV and Deflated Sharpe Ratio only when the number of trials, sample size and assumptions support meaningful interpretation. Explain their limitations rather than presenting them as automatic pass/fail scores.

Look-ahead diagnostics should include code-level checks and perturbation/sliced-run checks where feasible. A clean diagnostic applies only to the signals and samples actually exercised; untriggered signal paths remain unverified.

### 6.5 Failed performance gates

A zero-trade final holdout, below-baseline classifier or failed risk/performance gate is a valid negative result. Do not lower frozen SMC/Fibonacci/RSI/volume/spread/news/confidence thresholds, alter the final holdout, or cherry-pick a window to make the report pass.

## 7. Storage, orchestration and technology choices

These are staged decisions, not approvals to provision new services.

| Capability | Initial direction | Adoption gate |
|---|---|---|
| Research/task/run metadata | Existing Supabase Postgres with explicit owner/tenant columns and RLS | Migration, grants, policies, allow/deny SQL tests and A/B isolation review |
| Large research/data/model artifacts | Storage interface with immutable content hashes; Cloudflare R2 is a candidate | Bucket access policy, signed-download boundary, lifecycle/retention, quota/cost and recovery test |
| Job dispatch | No new queue in the foundation phase | A real external compute consumer, idempotency, bounded retries, acknowledgement, DLQ and duplicate-delivery tests |
| Quant compute | Separate Python process/container, not request-bound Worker code | Select runtime, resource limits, dependency pinning, sandboxing and operational ownership |
| Experiment tracking | First-party versioned run manifest and artifact references | Consider MLflow when run search/comparison/model lifecycle exceeds the minimal registry |
| Feature store | Keep feature schema and point-in-time lineage in the contract | Consider Feast only when historical feature retrieval and online/offline parity justify it |
| Stateful agent graph | Deterministic state machine initially | Consider LangGraph persistence only when durable pause/resume, recovery or human interrupts are required |

### 7.1 Cloudflare boundary

Cloudflare Workers are suitable for authentication-aware routing, contract validation, job creation and status reads. They are not the heavy historical simulation/training runtime: the Workers limits documentation currently lists 10 ms CPU time per request on Free, with 128 MB memory. The compute plane must be separately hosted.

Cloudflare Queues can later support off-request dispatch. A pull consumer can run outside Workers, including in another language/runtime, and must explicitly pull and acknowledge/retry messages. Configure a dead-letter queue before enabling a consumer; messages that exhaust retries without a DLQ can be deleted.

### 7.2 Supabase and object storage boundary

Supabase Postgres should store small metadata and access relationships; large files belong in object storage. Every customer-visible research table and artifact lookup must be owner/tenant scoped. RLS must be paired with least-privilege grants and tested allow/deny cases; a policy alone does not revoke an existing table grant.

R2 is a viable object-storage candidate because it exposes Workers and S3-compatible APIs and documents strong consistency. This does not itself decide bucket provisioning, retention, access control or billing.

### 7.3 Optional framework decisions

- **MLflow:** use its concepts of experiment/run grouping and logging parameters, code versions, metrics, dataset inputs and artifacts as a reference. Do not add a standalone server until model-training and registry needs justify its operational cost.
- **Feast:** point-in-time joins are relevant to prevent features created/backfilled after a decision from leaking into historical training. The contract must retain both event timestamp and created/availability timestamp even before a feature store is adopted.
- **LangGraph:** checkpoints/stores are useful when an agent must resume after interruption or recover thread state. The initial research workflow can use a deterministic state machine and immutable task records without adopting it.

## 8. Trust, security and tenant isolation

- Authenticate before research-task creation, source fetch, result listing, artifact download, cancellation or review action.
- Resolve the owner and tenant server-side. Every read/write query and object key is scoped to that identity; test Tenant A allow and Tenant B deny for metadata, source passages, run metrics and artifact download.
- Source content, repository files, market news, uploaded documents and model outputs are untrusted data. Treat embedded instructions as data; never execute source-provided code or let it expand the tool allowlist.
- The source-fetch layer must defend against SSRF: allow only supported schemes/providers; reject loopback, private, link-local and metadata-service destinations; revalidate every redirect; enforce content-type, response-size, timeout and redirect limits.
- Use bounded retrieval budgets, per-tenant quotas, idempotency keys, request correlation IDs and safe error codes. Do not persist raw auth headers or secret query parameters.
- Keep broker credentials and Vault plaintext entirely outside Deep Research and Quant Compute. Account context, where explicitly requested, must be sanitized and read-only.
- Audit who created a plan, changed its scope, launched an experiment, downloaded an artifact and recorded a review decision.

## 9. Research Window product responsibilities

The Research Window should make the lifecycle inspectable, not hide it behind a chat response.

Core views:

1. **Plan:** objective, research questions, constraints, versions, current stage and blockers.
2. **Sources:** source list, publisher/date, source snapshots, citations, retrieval warnings and access status.
3. **Evidence:** claim matrix, supporting sources, counter-evidence, contradictions and unverified items.
4. **Hypotheses:** falsifiable statement, alternatives, success/failure criteria and experiment links.
5. **Experiments:** dataset/code/strategy hashes, fold schedule, cost assumptions, queue/run status and failure reason.
6. **Results:** baseline comparison, fold/regime analysis, risk metrics, uncertainty, selection-bias diagnostics and downloadable artifacts.
7. **Review:** human decision, scope of approval, notes, expiry/version and rollback reference.

Status labels must reflect backend evidence. A spinner, generated plan or model response is not proof that a job is running or a research result is verified.

## 11. Definition of done

A Deep Research task is done only when the plan version, source snapshots, material claims, counter-evidence, hypothesis decisions, experiment manifests and final report are linked and reproducible.

A Quant run is done only when its exact inputs, data quality, code/runtime, strategy/feature versions, evaluation schedule, costs, metrics, artifacts and terminal status are persisted and validated. Partial folds, missing artifacts or an invalid manifest mean failed/blocked, not complete.

A candidate can advance only through recorded human review to an explicitly scoped paper/shadow stage. Live execution remains outside this engine.
